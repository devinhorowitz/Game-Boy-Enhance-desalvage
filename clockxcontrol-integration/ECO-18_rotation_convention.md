# ECO-18 — what this board means by zero degrees

**Board:** unchanged. No copper, no pads, no placements, no attributes.
**New:** two pin-1 views in `render/`, checks [18], one negative case.
**Closes:** the open item `pcbway-assembly/README.md` had carried since ECO-9 — *"the CPL's
rotation convention is unverified."*

---

## 18.1 The question, stated precisely

A position file carries one number per part. The line turns the part by it, from **their**
zero reference. Nothing in a netlist, a BOM, a DRC or a 3D render can tell you whether that
reference is the same as the board's — and if it is not, every polarised and every multi-pin
part goes in rotated.

This is answerable in two steps, and both are measurements rather than assumptions:

1. **Is the CPL's `rot` really KiCad's?** Ours is generated by `scripts/bom_split.py`, not by
   KiCad, so that needed proving rather than assuming.
2. **Does *this board's* zero match the standard one?** MouseBiteLabs drew his own footprint
   library. A footprint whose pin 1 sits in a different corner from the industry-standard land
   means the same `rot` puts the part somewhere else — silently.

## 18.2 Step one: the rotations are kicad-cli's, part by part

`kicad-cli pcb export pos` is KiCad's own exporter and the reference an assembler means by "a
KiCad position file". Exported from the shipped board and compared row by row:

```
kicad-cli rows: 181   our CPL rows: 180
x differs: 180   y differs: 180   rot differs: 0   (of 180)
```

**Zero rotation differences across all 180 parts.** The x/y differ only in datum — KiCad
measures from the page origin, ECO-17's columns from the board's lower-left corner, and the
two differ by a constant (0.22, −0.373) mm because MouseBiteLabs placed the board near the
page origin. Both are in the file; the header names each.

The 181st row is **`REF**`**, the unannotated crystal parked 9 mm off the board. **KiCad's own
exporter puts it in the position file.** ECO-16 found that footprint and check [13] warns about
it; this is independent confirmation that the warning describes something real rather than
hypothetical, from a second tool that has no idea what our checks think.

## 18.3 Step two: pin 1 is where the stock library puts it

Every placed footprint family was measured against KiCad's own `kicad-footprints`:

| Result | Families |
|---|---|
| pin 1 identical to stock, **0.000 mm** | 12 |
| same corner, land tweaked | 2 — `SOT-23` (0.050 mm), `L_CommonModeChoke_TDK_ACM2520-3P` (0.175 mm) |
| MouseBiteLabs' own, no stock equivalent | 7 — ledgered in `NO_STOCK_EQUIVALENT` |

**A land that differs is fine; a pin-1 corner that differs is what rotates a part.** Neither
outlier moves the corner — both are pads extended outward, which is a solderability choice.

So the board carries the stock KiCad convention, measured rather than assumed. The remaining
question is not "what does this board mean by 0°" but the ordinary one of confirming your
assembler takes a KiCad position file.

### The mistake this nearly became

The first pass at §18.3 reported **two families flipped a corner** — `MSOP-10_3x3mm_P0.5mm`
and the common-mode choke, both apparently mirrored against stock. That would have been a
serious finding.

It was wrong, and it was wrong because of how the comparison was written. Both families exist
only on `B.Cu`, and **KiCad bakes the back-side flip into the stored pad coordinates.** Proved
by taking families that appear on *both* sides and comparing their stored locals:

| family | pins identical | mirrored in Y |
|---|---|---|
| `SOT-23-5` (U3 / U8) | 1/5 | **5/5** |
| `TSOT-23-6` (Q2 / U11) | 2/6 | **6/6** |
| `TSSOP-14` (U16 / U7) | 2/14 | **14/14** |
| `SOT-23` (Q3 / Q1) | 1/3 | **3/3** |

Comparing a back-side instance against a front-side stock definition therefore reads as a
mirror every time. Un-mirror first and all 14 agree. The check does this, and says why.

The same trap bit the geometry earlier in this ECO: a first pin-1 bearing table applied an
extra mirror for `B.Cu` parts, which `geom.collect` proves is not needed — its pad positions
land on real back-side copper at **0.000 mm** at 0°, 90°, 180° and −90°. Twenty back-side
parts were wrong in that draft.

## 18.4 Part by part, against the renders

`render/agbm02_pin1_front.png` and `render/agbm02_pin1_back.png` mark **pin 1 on all 39 placed
parts whose rotation changes anything** — a dot on pin 1 and a stalk back to the placement
origin the CPL reports, labelled by refdes, polarised parts in a second colour. 16 on the
front, 23 on the back, 7 polarised.

**The other 131 placed parts are deliberately unmarked.** They are symmetric two-terminal
0603/0805 passives: at 0° and at 180° they are the same part in the same place. Marking them
would bury the 39 that matter under 131 that cannot be wrong.

The board is also internally consistent, which is a necessary condition and was checked
separately: **pin-1 bearing is a pure function of (package, rotation, side), with no
exceptions across all 39.**

## 18.5 The seven polarised parts, checked electrically

Orientation only matters because polarity does, so the polarised parts were checked against
what they are wired to rather than just drawn:

| Ref | Pin 1 | Pin 2 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `D1` | `Net-(BT1-+)` | `GND` | cathode to battery +, anode to ground — a **reverse-polarity clamp**, correct |
| `D2` | `/VFILT` | `VBATT` | cathode to the filtered rail, anode to the battery — series blocking, correct |
| `DL1` | `/D1A` | `/DL1A` | pin 2 is the anode, matching `DL2` |
| `DL2` | `Net-(DL2-K)` | `/DL2A` | the net name says it: **K** on pin 1, **A** on pin 2 |
| `CP2` | `U6-LOUT/BTL−` | `P3.2` + `R20` | **+ on the amplifier side**, which sits at mid-supply DC; jack side at 0 V. Correct |
| `CP3` | `U6-ROUT` | `P3.3` + `R19` | same, correct |
| `CP1` | `U6-BTL+` | `SP1.2` (speaker) | **orientation cannot help this one** — see below |

`CP1` bridges a BTL speaker output. Both terminals sit at mid-supply, so there is no DC bias
in either direction and the part is reverse-biased on half of every audio cycle whichever way
round it goes. That is the defect already recorded in `pcbway-assembly/README.md`, and this
ECO neither creates nor fixes it — it confirms that **rotating `CP1` is not the remedy**.

## 18.6 What the gate holds

**Check [18]**, two assertions:

* every placed footprint family still puts pin 1 where KiCad's stock library does, un-mirrored
  for side — so if a future edit moves a corner, the board stops claiming the standard
  convention *loudly* rather than silently;
* every CPL rotation still equals `kicad-cli`'s own export, re-derived live from the shipped
  board.

It needs `kicad-footprints` and `kicad-cli`. Without them it **says so and does not run**,
rather than passing vacuously — the same rule the rest of this repository uses for an
unreachable distributor and an absent renderer.

One negative case: move a footprint's pin 1 to the opposite corner and [18] fails. **21 cases,
0 blind.**

## 18.7 What this does not establish

It does not prove PCBWay's zero reference. It establishes that this board speaks stock KiCad,
which converts an unbounded question — *"what does each of 21 package families mean by 0°?"* —
into one bounded one: *"does the assembler take a KiCad position file as-is?"* Ask them that,
and if the answer needs qualifying, the pin-1 views are the per-part evidence to qualify it
against.

`CP1`–`CP3` still have **no polarity marking on the board**, which remains worth fixing before
an assembly order regardless of what the rotations say.
