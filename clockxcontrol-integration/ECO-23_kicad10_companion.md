## 23.1 Why two boards, and why the KiCad 9 one is still the original

ECO-22 established that KiCad 10 will not read this repository's board and KiCad 9 will not
read the user's: **`Failed to load board`**, in that direction, from a KiCad 9.0.9
`kicad-cli` handed a version-20260206 file.

The two formats are not a superset and a subset. They are different encodings:

| KiCad 9 — 20241229 | KiCad 10 — 20260206 | |
|---|---|---|
| `(net 12)` | `(net "/CPU/TP8")` | segments and vias |
| `(net 12 "/CPU/TP8")` | `(net "/CPU/TP8")` | pads |
| a `(net N "name")` table at the top | **no table at all** | |
| `(drill 1)` | `(drill 1 (offset 0 0))` | through-hole pads |
| — | `(capping)` `(covering)` `(plugging)` | every via |

`build_board.py` produces this fork by **splicing text into MouseBiteLabs' KiCad 9 board**,
which is the only reason check [1] can assert the shipped file rebuilds *byte-for-byte*
from his committed zip. That property is the spine of this repository: every other check is
worth what it is worth because the board is a pure function of inputs that are all in git.
A KiCad 10 board cannot be produced that way — his base is KiCad 9 — so making KiCad 10 the
source of truth would put a seam in that spine.

**So the KiCad 9 file stays the original and the KiCad 10 file is a derived artifact,
exactly like the renders:** generated by a script, committed, and gated by a check that
re-derives it.

## 23.2 "The same copper" cannot mean byte equality, or even segment equality

KiCad 10 **merges collinear tracks**. The user's own save turned 3,554 segments into 3,243
without moving a micron of copper — and the first comparison run in ECO-22 read that as
*314 deleted tracks*. A gate that compares segment lists would produce the same wrong
answer every time someone opened the board.

So tracks are compared by **coverage**. Every segment is grouped by
`(layer, net, width, and the infinite line it lies on)`, projected onto that line, and the
intervals merged into maximal runs:

```
key   = (layer, net, unit direction with a canonical sign, signed offset from origin, width)
value = the merged, sorted set of intervals along that line
```

Two boards match when their run sets are identical. That is **invariant under merging and
splitting** — the thing KiCad 10 actually does — and still catches a track that moved,
changed net, changed layer, changed width, or vanished. On this board the 3,554 segments
reduce to **3,353 runs**, and both files produce the same 3,353.

Footprints, pads, vias and nets are compared exactly, with pad nets resolved to **names** on
both sides so the numbering change is invisible to the comparison rather than fatal to it.

## 23.3 The gate runs where KiCad does not

The **conversion** needs KiCad 10 (`pcbnew.LoadBoard` / `SaveBoard`) and cannot run on this
project's deliberately bare CI runner. The **proof** is pure string handling, so it runs
everywhere:

* `python3 scripts/kicad10.py` — convert and verify, needs KiCad 10
* `python3 scripts/kicad10.py --check` — verify the committed pair, needs nothing
* check **[19]** calls the same `kicad10.compare()`, so the tool that writes the file and
  the gate that accepts it cannot disagree about what "the same copper" means
* `.github/workflows/consistency.yml` runs `--check` on every push

Check [19] also refuses a companion whose file version is not *newer* than the source's,
which is what a stale copy looks like after someone edits the KiCad 9 board and forgets.
`test_checks.py` case 26 moves one track endpoint in the KiCad 9 board and requires [19] to
say `track coverage differs`.

## 23.4 The companion ships with his rules, not KiCad's

`pcbnew.SaveBoard()` also drops a `.kicad_pro` and a `.kicad_prl` beside the board it
writes — and **the `.kicad_pro` it writes is a fresh KiCad 10 default**. That is precisely
the file ECO-22 traced the phantom 710 to: `min_hole_to_hole` 0.25 against his 0.5,
`min_clearance` 0.0 against his 0.15, `min_resolved_spokes` 2 against his 1, six of his
`ignore` severities promoted to `warning`, and none of his 113 `drc_exclusions`.

Shipping the KiCad 10 board next to that would hand the recipient the same wrong answer we
just spent an ECO diagnosing. So `convert()` **overwrites it with his project immediately**,
and the `.prl` — per-user UI state, open layers and zoom — is deleted and gitignored.

Both boards therefore ship with `min_hole_to_hole 0.5`, `min_clearance 0.15`,
`silk_overlap ignore` and all 113 exclusions, under their own stems, so whichever one you
open finds its rules where KiCad looks for them.

## 23.5 Corroboration

The KiCad 10 companion, re-poured and run through `check_drc.py` against his project file,
reports **204 violations and 0 unconnected** — the same 204 and the same 0 as the KiCad 9
board, type for type:

```
solder_mask_bridge 8   copper_edge_clearance 8   courtyards_overlap 5   shorting_items 2
clearance 47           items_not_allowed 132     via_dangling 2
```

Two independent readers — this repository's text comparison and KiCad's own design-rule
engine — agree the two files are the same board.
